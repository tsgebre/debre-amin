The Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church is one of the Oriental Orthodox churches. It shares the same faith as the Coptic, Syrian and Armenian Orthodox churches.

The Church traces the first seed of her faith to the pages of the New Testament. The Acts of the Apostles (Acts 8:26–39) tells how the Ethiopian eunuch — a court official and treasurer of the Kandake, the queen of the Ethiopians — was baptized by Saint Philip on the road from Jerusalem to Gaza. He is remembered as the first Ethiopian Christian, who carried the good news home after the Ascension of our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ.

Centuries later, in the ancient kingdom of Aksum under King Ezana, Christianity became the faith of the royal court around the year 330 — and it was then that the Church received her first bishop: Frumentius, consecrated bishop of Aksum by Athanasius of Alexandria. Ethiopians remember him as Abba Salama, Kesate Birhan: "Father of Peace, Revealer of Light."

In the late fifth century the Nine Saints came to Aksum. They translated Scripture and other religious books into Ge'ez. Ge'ez is still the Church's liturgical language today, and the readings and parts of the liturgy are also given in Amharic.

In 1959 Abune Basilios was enthroned as the first Patriarch of Ethiopia.

The word *Tewahedo* is Ge'ez for "made one." It expresses the Church's faith in the union of divinity and humanity in Christ. The Church's Bible is counted as 81 books: 46 in the Old Testament and 35 in the New Testament.
