The Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church is one of the Oriental Orthodox churches. It shares the same faith as the Coptic, Syrian and Armenian Orthodox churches.

Its roots reach back to the ancient kingdom of Aksum. There, under King Ezana, Christianity became the faith of the royal court around the year 330. Frumentius was consecrated the first bishop of Aksum by Athanasius of Alexandria. Ethiopians remember him as Abba Salama, Kesate Birhan: "Father of Peace, Revealer of Light."

In the late fifth century the Nine Saints came to Aksum. They translated Scripture and other religious books into Ge'ez. Ge'ez is still the Church's liturgical language today, and the readings and parts of the liturgy are also given in Amharic.

In 1959 Abune Basilios was enthroned as the first Patriarch of Ethiopia.

The word *Tewahedo* is Ge'ez for "made one." It expresses the Church's faith in the union of divinity and humanity in Christ. The Church's Bible is counted as 81 books: 46 in the Old Testament and 35 in the New Testament.
